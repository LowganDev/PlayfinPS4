# Playfin 2.2

## Intent

2.2.0 lets people make Playfin look the way they like, and fills two gaps every other Jellyfin client has
closed: a preview picture while seeking (Jellyfin's trickplay) and the playback speed. The themes come from the
Jellyfin community's best-known looks for jellyfin-web, rebuilt natively, so that someone who uses one of them
in the browser finds the same mood on the PS4.

The HDMI-CEC remote (planned as 2.1.2) waits: the user has no TV that supports it. 2.1.1's goal (https channels
played directly) shipped in 2.1.0.

## Decisions (the user's, 2026-11-10)

### Themes

- Six themes in Settings › Affichage › Thème, a new row above "Couleur d'accent", choosing from a list like the
  accent's: **Projecteur** (today's look, the default), **ElegantFin**, **Ultrachromic**, **Abyss**, **Zesty**,
  **Finimalism**. The choice applies at once (as the accent does today) and is kept across restarts.
- A theme changes the look, never the layout: the background, the surface and text colours, the cards' corner
  radius and border, how the focused item stands out, the navigation tabs' style, and its font. Screens keep the
  same places for everything.
- The accent stays a setting of its own, on top of any theme. Each theme has its own accent; picking a theme
  sets the accent back to that theme's, and the user may then choose another one in "Couleur d'accent".
- Each theme has its own font (as in the mockups), not Sora everywhere.
- No code, image or logo is taken from the themes' repositories: only their colours, shapes and mood, as listed
  below. Each theme is credited by name with its author, repository and licence in Settings › Licences.
- The release does not wait for the themes' authors: writing to ElegantFin's and Zesty's authors is a courtesy,
  and the themes ship under their names with the credit either way.
- Mockups the user approved: the "Thèmes Playfin 2.2" page (Projecteur's screenshot, then one mockup of the home
  screen per theme beside the original's screenshot).

### Preview while seeking (trickplay)

- While the user seeks with ← → or L1 / R1 (including the accelerated hold), a picture of the frame at the
  target time shows above the progress bar, with that time under it, so they see where they will land before
  the seek applies.
- The pictures are the server's trickplay images (Jellyfin 10.9 and later, when the library has them enabled).
- When the server has none, nothing changes from today and nothing tells the user (no warning they did not ask
  for).
- No preview for files played without the server (downloads, USB drives) or for live channels.

### Playback speed

- A "Vitesse" choice in the player's menu, beside audio and subtitles: 0.5×, 0.75×, 1×, 1.25×, 1.5×, 2×.
- The voice keeps its pitch.
- Works in direct play, through the server's transcode and for files played without the server (downloads, USB
  drives). Not for live channels. Music keeps normal speed.
- Above 1×, the stream is not changed: when the console cannot decode fast enough (it decodes 1080p at only
  24-25 frames a second, docs/ps4-notes.md), mpv drops pictures and the sound stays right and in step. The
  user chose this over asking the server for a lighter stream.
- The "Se termine à" time in the bar takes the speed into account.
- The speed carries over to the next episode while episodes follow one another (the next-episode card, L2 / R2,
  the end of an episode); a video started from the menus starts at 1×.

## Goals

- Six themes that each read as their original at a glance from a couch, in every screen (home, libraries,
  details, search, downloads, settings, music, Live TV, dialogs and menus, the hints bar).
- Trickplay previews that appear without delay while seeking on the console.
- Playback speed that keeps audio and picture in step (pictures dropped above 1× when the console cannot keep
  up).

## Non-goals

- No light theme, no user-made or downloadable themes, no per-theme icons, sounds or layouts.
- The video player's own bar and overlays keep today's layout and dark overlay whatever the theme (no theme
  background behind the video); they take the theme's font and the current accent like the rest of the app, so
  no second set of colours or fonts is needed (builder's call). The menus opened from the player (tracks,
  picture, Vitesse) are the app's usual choice dialogs and follow the theme; so does the seek preview.
- No SyncPlay, lyrics, subtitle styling, sleep timer or HDMI-CEC in 2.2.0.

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- The interface dropping below its steady 30 frames a second on the console in any theme (2.0 got there with
  sprites for fades and a baked backdrop shade; the themes must keep that).
- Text hard to read from a couch in any theme, or a focus that is hard to see.
- Shipping anything copied from the themes' repositories (CSS, images, logos, wallpapers), or brand-named
  schemes (some themes mimic streaming services: not those).
- A message or badge the user did not ask for (no "trickplay unavailable", no "theme by").

## Constraints

- Playfin's rules: C++17 (OpenOrbis clang 12 + libc++), `-Werror`, no `std::filesystem` in product code, writes
  through `core::dataPath()`, tests first, Linux before the console, screenshots shown before any console
  deployment, French UI in "vous", 14 languages, never copy code from Switchfin, borealis, Playtube or another
  client.
- Software rendering on the console: a gradient or blur drawn every frame goes through the per-pixel shader and is
  several times slower than an image drawn 1:1 (`ui/sprites.hpp`). Theme backgrounds are baked into images when
  they change (as the halo is rebuilt on `themeGeneration()`, and as `BackdropShade` bakes its shade), never
  drawn as live gradients or blurs. A blurred backdrop may be made by drawing a very small copy of the picture
  stretched with filtering.
- Fonts: each added face must be under the SIL Open Font License (or as free), with its licence file in
  `resources/fonts` and in the licences page. Today `nvg_backend.cpp` picks Sora, or Noto Sans for Russian,
  Ukrainian and Vietnamese, plus Noto Sans CJK; a theme's font falls back the same way: when it lacks the UI
  language's script, the whole UI uses Noto Sans in that language, as Sora does today (no per-character mixing;
  builder's call).
  Only the weights the UI uses (regular, semibold, bold).
- Trickplay sheets are large once decoded (a 10 × 10 sheet of 320-pixel pictures is about 3200 × 1800): keep in
  memory only what the preview needs (for example the pictures cut out of a sheet, or the current sheet) and fetch
  ahead so the preview keeps up with a held seek. Changes to low-level memory management need the user's
  approval first (rule 4); an ordinary cache does not.
- Theme ids saved in `settings.json` are neutral (`projecteur`, `slate`, `chroma`, `abyss`, `mint`, `violet`),
  so that a theme's display name can change later without breaking saved settings (builder's call).
- Every setting read from `settings.json` keeps working for people coming from 2.1 (an accent they chose stays,
  with Projecteur).
- Work happens on a branch named `2.2` once the user agrees to create it; nothing is committed, merged or
  published without the user's agreement each time.

## Left to the builder

- How themes are represented (a table of tokens, a struct per theme) and how `ui::Theme`, `applyAccent` and the
  accent list ("Couleur du thème" first) change, as long as the decisions above hold.
- The exact blur, darkening and gradient values within the ranges above, judged on the console.
- How Ultrachromic, Abyss, Zesty and Finimalism pick their backdrop: the focused card's backdrop on the home and
  library screens, the item's on its details page; what screens without an item show (the last backdrop or the
  plain background); how a change of backdrop fades in without costing frames.
- The trickplay width chosen (the smallest that looks right on a 1080p TV), the cache size, the preview's size
  and place above the bar.
- Whether the speed is reported to the server, if Jellyfin's playback reports have a field for it.
- The fonts' exact files (static weights or one variable face).

## Testing

- Unit tests: theme tokens (every theme defines every colour; text against its surface keeps a readable
  contrast), the accent reset on a theme change, the settings round trip, trickplay metadata parsing and the
  picture chosen for a time, the speed menu and its carry-over to the next episode, "Se termine à" at 1.5×.
- UI references: the home and the settings in each theme (`scripts/update-ui-refs.sh`), the seek preview, the
  speed menu.
- On the console (after the reminders: back up saves and settings, never cut the power during an install, prefer
  external storage): every theme on the home, a library, a details page and the settings at 30 frames a second;
  trickplay on a film from the user's server with its images enabled; 1.5× and 2× on a 1080p episode (sound in step,
  pictures dropped) through to the next one.
